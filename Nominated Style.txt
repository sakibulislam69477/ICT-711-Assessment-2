4.1 Nominated Style
In Section 2.2, three candidate styles were evaluated in relation to the architectural drivers, including a modular monolith, fine-grained microservices and event-driven, service-driven. The decision is not based on the preferences of the technology, but on the quality feature [1].
EcoGrid advocates for an architecture based on services and events. The platform is divided into three bounded contexts from Section 3.1, each of which has its own service that can be deployed. The platform consists of three bounded contexts from 3.1 for Smart Meter Integration, Marketplace, and Financial Settlement services, and they are coarse-grained and independently deployable. The services are connected by a message broker, and every one has its own data store (as shown in figure 1). Event-driven designs isolate producers from consumers, and are well suited to workloads that have high churn and data velocity such as IoT telemetry [2]. However, the services are not fine-grained, much less one per entity, since the overhead of operations is disproportionately large for a small team [10], [11], [12].
Distancing Marketplace from meter ingestion. Only the Smart Meter Integration service will be given the raw readings. It validates them and aggregates them, sending summarized events e.g. HourlySurplusCalculated to the broker. The Marketplace has nothing to do with these events, and does not query the meter data store. When they increase in number, then the readings are also waiting in the ingestion pipeline, and don't block any Marketplace resources such as CPU, database connections or network.
Handling high-frequency data. Bursts are buffered by the broker and ingestion consumers can be horizontally scaled while not horizontally scaling the other services [2]. Workloads of the ingestion can be scaled up and down without changing the Marketplace or Settlement.
Protecting financial integrity. Settlement is called synchronously by the Marketplace to reserve the required funds when a trade is matched, following the context map. Later steps are realized using events and events with a compensating action using idempotency keys to avoid getting duplicate charges [3], [13]. Settlement owns its own ledger that holds the most high fleshed domain separated from any other failure [7].
Working well as a team player. They follow Conway's Law [8], [9] with the three services having three ownership groups as shown in Section 3.3. At most times there can be only one team owning the service.
Evolvability. Within a service, there are carefully defined boundaries and modules for code. If the service fails to meet a measurable requirement, like a failed fitness function [14] then it is divided further. Publishers do not have to change their services to add new capabilities such as notifications/dynamic pricing as they are new event subscribers and can be negotiated for a subscription without modifying the publisher service
